# CollegeNotes{11:11} — experience proposal

Status: Phase 2 proposal, not selected design or working application. AUTH-1-2 authorizes preparation this pass; another chat AI generates images for the owner's review. The original thirteen screenshots are not required. Use clearly labeled synthetic study content for images and fixtures.

The central experience is a readable study passage with one useful next action. A returning learner should recognize the course, see where they stopped, and resume in one action. Source evidence and controls remain available without competing with the reading. Courses can accumulate over years without turning the home page into an assignment dashboard.

Proposed priority order: current task and course; readable source; immediate next action; progress/recovery status; optional context/tools. Show one primary action in a region. Hide complexity behind named secondary panels, not unexplained icons. No forced setup tour, timed onboarding, streak pressure or claim that time spent proves learning.

Global navigation: course switcher, Home, Sources, Study, Practice, Requirements, Progress; Settings at the foot. Add material is always available in the Sources context and from empty Home. Reading opens within Sources. Back preserves the last source position. A course switch saves the current task and offers the other course's own resume point; no cross-course tutor context leaks.

On first use, an honest empty home shows “Add your first course” and a short explanation: “Bring your screenshots, documents, text and notes.” Course creation asks only for a name; term and instructor are optional. Adding material can follow immediately. Existing study work is never overwritten by an import.

Returning home shows “Continue where you stopped” with course, source title, section and activity state. Secondary choices are “Add material” and “Choose something else.” An optional session length is a suggestion, never a timer that dismisses work. Progress shows attempts and recall evidence separately from reading completion.

The reader has a centered prose column, section navigation and an optional context drawer. Source view, personal notes, internet research evidence and tutor explanation have distinct headings. Listening controls stay near the passage, with a clear pause/stop action. A question pauses narration and preserves position; return to reading is explicit. A document correction shows original and extracted text side by side, with an accessible tabbed fallback at the minimum desktop window or 200% zoom.

COMM 110 practice uses Praise / Question / Polish to help the learner classify observations and write their own response. The app can teach the method, track requirements and record rehearsal. It cannot generate prohibited outlines or assignment sources/citations. Source references inside study are navigational evidence, not generated assignment citations. Missing course facts read “Not provided yet.”

Offline status is specific: “Ready offline: 3 sources and 2 audio tracks” or “This audio isn't downloaded.” The app preserves queued work and explains the available local action. Unavailable tutoring cannot erase a typed question or make a cached response look newly generated.

Performance and accessibility are design constraints: conventional 2D navigation, text outside canvas, keyboard access, reduced motion, and a graphics-disabled equivalent. A selective visual aid must answer a learning question. No virtual campus, navigable world, ocean, decorative 3D or background simulation.
